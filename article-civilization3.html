Title

Enter a concise, descriptive, and informative title. Avoid abbreviations or complex formulas. The title should clearly convey the main focus of your research.

Keywords

List 4–6 relevant keywords separated by commas to help improve discoverability of your article in academic searches and databases. Example: artificial intelligence, governance, ethics, democracy, transparency, policy.

Abstract

Provide a comprehensive summary of your research in 150–300 words. Include the following elements:

  • Purpose: State the main objective of your study
  • Methods: Briefly describe your research methodology
  • Findings: Summarize key results and outcomes
  • Conclusions: State the main conclusions and implications

Introduction

Begin by introducing your research topic and establishing its significance. Provide context for why this research matters and what gap it addresses in existing literature. Include relevant citations to background work and clearly define your research objectives. The introduction should draw readers in and establish the foundation for your entire study.

Key Elements:

  • Topic background and context
  • Significance and relevance
  • Literature review highlights
  • Research objectives and questions

Materials and Methods

Outline your research methodology in sufficient detail to allow other researchers to reproduce your study. Describe the materials used, experimental setup, procedures followed, and data collection methods. This section should be clear, comprehensive, and organized logically.

Subsections to include:

  • Study design and approach
  • Participants or subjects
  • Materials and tools
  • Data collection procedures
  • Analysis methods

Results

Present your research findings clearly and objectively. Use tables, figures, graphs, and charts to display data effectively. This section should focus solely on presenting results without interpretation. Let the data speak for itself, and refer to supporting figures and tables as appropriate.

Presentation guidelines:

  • Organize results logically by research question
  • Use clear, descriptive table and figure captions
  • Avoid interpretation and analysis here
  • Reference all tables and figures in text

Discussion

Interpret your results in the context of your research questions and existing literature. Discuss the implications of your findings, compare them with similar studies, and address any limitations. Explain what your results mean and why they matter for your field.

Discussion points:

  • Interpretation of main findings
  • Comparison with existing literature
  • Implications and applications
  • Limitations of the study
  • Suggestions for future research

Conclusion

Summarize the main contributions of your research. Restate your key findings and their significance. Avoid introducing new data or analysis. Clearly articulate the implications of your work and its contribution to the field. End with a strong closing statement that reinforces the importance of your research.

References

List all sources cited in your article using a consistent citation format. Common formats include APA, MLA, Chicago, and IEEE. Ensure all references are properly formatted and include complete bibliographic information.

Example references (APA format):

  • Author, A. A., & Author, B. B. (Year). Title of article. Journal Name, Volume(Issue), Page range.
  • Author, A. A. (Year). Title of book. Publisher.
